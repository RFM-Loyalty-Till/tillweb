# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two people look at the same screen at once.

- **RFM Loyalty sales reps** open the deal calculator on a phone or tablet while standing at a restaurant or cafe counter. They type in the owner's monthly card sales and the rate another bank has offered, then turn the screen around. They use it many times a week and need it to be fast with one hand.
- **Restaurant and cafe owners in the UAE** read the result over the rep's shoulder. They are busy, sceptical of sales tools, and comparing a lower card rate from a bank against RFM's bundle. They are not finance people; every label has to be plain.

The rest of this repository is the Till marketing website (Next.js, Tailwind, coral and navy brand). The deal calculator lives at `/deal-calculator` and is the product surface this file was written for.

## Product Purpose

RFM Loyalty charges a slightly higher card processing rate than competing banks, but supplies the payment devices together with its own products: Till (restaurant management), Till Go (handheld ordering on the payment device), a loyalty programme, OOHUP in-venue screens and, through Following, 15 influencers. A competing bank supplies only the card machines.

The calculator shows the owner, in their own numbers, that the rate difference is a small yearly amount next to what the same products would cost from outside suppliers. Success is an owner who understands the comparison in under a minute and trusts that the numbers were not rigged: it must report honestly when the other route is cheaper.

Outputs: the rate difference per month, year, day and per AED 100 bill; total yearly cost side by side; the verdict; the break-even card volume; a text summary to copy; a PDF to leave behind.

## Brand Personality

Calm, exact, plain-spoken.

It should feel like a well-made instrument a trusted adviser brings to a meeting, not a pitch deck. Confidence comes from clear numbers and restraint. The reference points the owner gave are Pip (pipdot.app: near-white ground, near-black type, one small orange accent, generous space, almost no boxes) and the Inspora archive (white, black, quiet neutral controls).

## Anti-references

- Anything that reads as generated by AI: cream or beige page with a serif or rounded display face and a terracotta accent, a card around every block, numbered pill badges on every section, small uppercase kickers, soft drop shadows under bordered cards, gradient headlines, emoji as icons.
- SaaS landing page tropes: hero metric tiles, three identical feature cards, glass panels.
- Bank brochure styling: navy and gold, stock photography of handshakes.
- A cluttered quote form that looks like an insurance comparison site.

## Design Principles

1. **The owner's number is the page.** Everything on screen exists to make one figure (better or worse off per year) believable. Nothing competes with it.
2. **Show the working.** Every total can be traced to the lines above it. Trust comes from being able to check the sum.
3. **Honest in both directions.** When the other route costs less, say so in the same calm voice. Never tint, hide or soften a result that goes against RFM.
4. **Fast with one thumb.** The rep is standing and holding the device. Inputs are large, in a sensible order, and the answer stays in view while typing.
5. **Quiet until it matters.** Colour and weight are spent on the result and on what the user is touching, not on decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA: text contrast 4.5:1 or better, visible keyboard focus, every input labelled, touch targets at least 44px.
- Works from 360px wide to desktop with no horizontal scrolling.
- Respects reduced motion.
- Numbers use tabular figures and AED with thousands separators. English only for now; Arabic is a likely later addition, so avoid layouts that cannot mirror.
